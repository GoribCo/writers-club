The river does not know my name,
but it walks beside me anyway —
past the library lights,
past the bicycles asleep in rows.

In Lagos the night is loud and warm.
Here it is quiet, and it listens.
I tell it about my mother's kitchen.
It answers with fog.

Maybe that is how a city learns you:
one cold step,
one borrowed word,
one river at a time.
