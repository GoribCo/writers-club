The envelope was too thick for a bill and too thin for a parcel. Jonas held it up to the kitchen light like it was evidence.

"Who still sends letters?" he asked.

"My grandmother," I said, and I knew before I opened it that it would smell of mustard oil and the cupboard where she keeps her good paper.

Inside there were three pages in her careful handwriting, and one dried flower from the tree outside our house in Sylhet. Jonas went quiet. Then he went to his room and came back with a pen, and asked me how to write his grandmother's address.
