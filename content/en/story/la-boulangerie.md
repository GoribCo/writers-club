On the first morning I went looking for a baguette. It's ridiculous, I know. You don't go abroad to find exactly what you left behind. But I was hungry, and hunger is not reasonable.

The bakery on the corner of my street is simply called "Bäckerei". Behind the counter, a woman with red cheeks watched me search for a long minute.

"Baguette?" I asked, full of hope.

She handed me something long, pale and soft. It was not a baguette. It was the idea of a baguette, described by someone who had never seen one.

Then she put down a dark loaf next to it, heavy as a stone, covered in seeds.

"Probieren," she said. *Try it.*

I tried it that evening, with butter and a little salt. It was dense, sour, generous. It was nothing like home, and that was exactly what made it good.

Now I go every Saturday. She saves me the last rye loaf. I taught her to say "croissant" with the accent. She still refuses.
